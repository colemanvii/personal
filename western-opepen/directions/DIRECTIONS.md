# Three competing directions

Board: [`three-directions-board.jpg`](three-directions-board.jpg). Every image here is
an AI composition study, not artwork.

## A — SECTION LINE · the silhouette is land

**Premise.** The American West was surveyed onto an 8×8-like grid before it was settled;
Opepen is a figure made on an 8×8 grid. Put one on the other. The figure is never drawn
*on* the land — it is found *in* it: two pivot circles (one with a squared corner), a row
field, a farmstead; a pen, a wall, a fence; a specimen's place in a field book; a
horizon drawn through a faint construction line.

**Visual system.** Plan view and plat conventions at the top of the set descending to
ground-level field drawing at the bottom. One ink, iron-oxide red. Survey marginalia
(stations, chains, bearings, weather) handwritten.

**Relationship to the silhouette.** Territory. The geometry is a human imposition on
land; every drawing negotiates between the ruled figure and the unruled ground.

Studies: S01, S06, S10 (and all 12 in `../studies/`).

## B — SPECIMEN · the silhouette is built from things

**Premise.** Western material culture and natural history physically *construct* the
four forms: lariat coils for eyes, saddle-blanket weave for the mouth, horseshoes for
the torso; pressed grasses; a brand burned into hide.

**Visual system.** Dense lithographic object drawing, specimen-plate layout, labels.

**Relationship.** Assemblage. The subject is the material of the figure.

Studies: B1 lariat · B2 brand · B3 pressed grasses.

**Verdict.** The most immediately likeable and the weakest. Every piece is a visual pun
("the eye is a coiled rope!"), and puns are spent on first reading. B also fights the
geometry: coils, hide and grass all want to round the left eye's corner (B1 did, in its
first render). Eighty of these is a catalog of props. Keep one or two as citations
inside A (WO-10-09 *Brand Book*, WO-20-13 *Barbed Wire*), where they are archival
objects rather than costumes.

## C — DAYBOOK · the silhouette is a ritual

**Premise.** Draw the figure once a day, outdoors, freehand, in one continuous line;
let wind, cold, light and the body record themselves. No illustration at all — the
mark is the subject. Eighty days.

**Visual system.** Single line, sometimes overlaid (C1: thirty days on one sheet), with a
pencilled log of date and conditions.

**Relationship.** Gesture. The figure is constant; the world deforms the hand.

Studies: C1 thirty days · C2 wind · C3 eyes closed.

**Verdict.** The most honest about authorship and the most conceptually clean, but it
throws away what Cole is best at (observation, animals, land, illustration), and eighty
near-identical outlines would make the 1/40 the dullest group in the set. Its method,
though, is exactly right for the 1/40 — so it survives inside A as the discipline of
*The Back Forty* (drawn on site, conditions logged, some single-line, one blind).

## Recommendation: A, absorbing C's method and a little of B's density

SECTION LINE is the only premise in which the Opepen geometry and the subject share an
origin. It isn't a theme laid over the figure: the grid is the subject. That gives:

- **A reason to exist inside Opepen.** It is about drawing on a grid — Opepen's own
  condition — seen through the most consequential grid in American history.
- **Range without incoherence.** Aerial, elevation, section, plan, specimen, horizon;
  dense to almost blank; figurative to abstract — all justified by one rule.
- **A structure collectors can hold.** The six groups are six scales of attention. The
  forty 1/40 stations, two chains apart, walk exactly one mile around a forty-acre
  square. 80 artworks = 80 chains = one section line.
- **The contradictions of the West, without slogans.** The same grid built homesteads
  and dismantled reservations; the set shows the grid's handwriting and lets it speak.
- **An exhibition ten years from now.** The 1/40 hangs as a ring around the room — the
  viewer walks the mile — with the 1/1 at the centre.

**Where I am challenging you.**

1. Retire the working title "Western Opepen". It is a genre label. "Section Line" is an
   idea.
2. No cowboys. No hats. People only by their evidence. The Western clichés are
   already in the viewer's head — the work is more powerful for refusing them.
3. Red must mean something. Use an **iron-oxide** red (PR101/PR102 — literally ground
   earth, and lightfast), and for the 1/1 make ink from soil from the land being drawn.
4. Your current red-ink hand is a flash-sheet hand. This set needs three speeds of line;
   the slow ones will be the hardest and the most important.
5. Choose a **real** section and a **real** forty, and draw on site. The concept only
   works if the land exists — and the surveyor's original GLO plat for it becomes part of
   the provenance.

## The six edition groups

| Group | Title | Purpose | Viewpoint | Family resemblance |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1/1 | **One Square Mile** | The key: the whole system at once. Most hours, largest sheet, earth-made ink. | Aerial plat | Grid + figure as fields |
| 1/4 | **Quarter Section** | Time. The same 160 acres through Breaking, Stand, Windrows, After the Blow. | Aerial plan | Same parcel, four mark vocabularies |
| 1/5 | **Improvements** | Human evidence: Dwelling, Well, Fence, Dugout, Grave — what a homesteader swore to, plus what wasn't listed. | Elevation / section | Figure as structure, void, boundary |
| 1/10 | **Stock** | The animals, drawn to sporting-illustration standard; behaviour proves geometry. | Plan to close elevation | Animals in, around, or forming the figure |
| 1/20 | **Field Notes** | Natural history along the line: plants, birds, insects, found objects. | Specimen plate | Vignette, construction line, life size |
| 1/40 | **The Back Forty** | Presence. Forty stations, two chains apart, around one forty; drawn on site, logged. | Ground level, horizon in the neck | Horizon in the neck gap; station marginalia |

Shared across all 80: exact geometry from the template; one iron-oxide ink (plus graphite
construction where it is part of the image); warm paper; handwritten marginalia; no
costume; each must pass the editorial standard (`../editorial/STANDARD.md`).

**The 1/40 is not the cheap seats.** It holds half the collectors, so it carries the
most rigorous rule (fixed stations, logged conditions, drawn on site) and the widest
emotional range (fog, fire, moonrise, a dead calf, the blind drawing). Each 1/40 holder
owns a real, findable point on the earth.
