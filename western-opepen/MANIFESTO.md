# SECTION LINE

*Eighty drawings by Cole Calfee. Working manifesto, draft 1 — November 2026.*

The American West was drawn before it was lived in.

In 1785 Congress ordered the land west of the Ohio cut into a grid: townships six
miles square, sections one mile square, quarter sections of 160 acres, forties of
40. Surveyors walked it with a chain sixty-six feet long. Eighty chains make a mile.
The grid went down over prairie, river, burial ground and buffalo range alike, and
everything that came after — the homestead, the fence, the pivot, the county road —
was built along its lines. Fly over Kansas today and you are looking at a drawing.

Opepen is also a figure made on a grid: eight units by eight, two eyes, a mouth, a
torso. One eye is a circle. The other has a single square corner. From the air the
High Plains are full of that shape: a center-pivot circle laid inside a quarter
section, one corner planted square.

This set puts the two grids on top of each other and draws what happens.

**The rules**

1. Every work is drawn by hand on paper, in one ink: iron-oxide red — a pigment that
   is literally ground earth. The digital file is a faithful scan of a real drawing.
   Nothing is generated, traced from generated images, or "fixed" in software.
2. The Opepen geometry is never approximated. It is laid in from the exact template
   and then *found* in the land: as a field, a pen, a wall, a fence, a specimen, a
   horizon. It is never a costume. No hats, no boots, no cowboy.
3. People appear only by their evidence: furrows, wire, a grave, a lit window.
4. Each of the eighty must be worth framing to someone who has never heard of Opepen.
5. The land's contradictions stay in the drawing. The grid made homes; it also took
   them. The set does not resolve that and does not decorate it.

**The structure: a walk from altitude to the ground**

| Group | Title | Scale of attention |
|---|---|---|
| 1/1 | One Square Mile | the section, from the air |
| 1/4 | Quarter Section | 160 acres, four seasons |
| 1/5 | Improvements | the structures a homesteader built to prove up |
| 1/10 | Stock | the animals |
| 1/20 | Field Notes | specimens collected along the line |
| 1/40 | The Back Forty | forty stations, two chains apart, one mile around a forty |

The perimeter of a forty-acre square is exactly eighty chains — one mile. Its forty
stations hold the forty most-held drawings. Eighty works; eighty chains; one section
line.
