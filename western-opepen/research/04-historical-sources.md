## The grid and the land (the premise)

- **Land Ordinance of 1785** — establishes the rectangular survey: 6-mile townships of
  36 one-mile sections. Primary text: Journals of the Continental Congress, Library of
  Congress (memory.loc.gov, *A Century of Lawmaking*).
- **Public Land Survey System and Gunter's chain** — 1 chain = 66 ft = 4 rods;
  80 chains = 1 mile; 10 square chains = 1 acre. Section = 640 acres; quarter section =
  160; quarter-quarter ("forty") = 40 acres, 20 chains on a side, **80-chain (1-mile)
  perimeter**. Primary: BLM *Manual of Surveying Instructions*; original survey plats
  and surveyors' field notes at the BLM General Land Office Records site
  (glorecords.blm.gov). *Action: find the original GLO plat and field notes for the
  section we draw — the surveyor's own handwriting becomes a source for our marginalia.*
- **Homestead Act, 1862** — 160 acres; title after five years' residence and
  "improvements". Primary: National Archives, Milestone Documents. Homestead case files
  (National Archives) list the improvements claimants swore to — dwelling, well, fencing,
  acres broken. *These lists are the literal source of the 1/5 group.*
- **Dawes (General Allotment) Act, 1887** — the same 160-acre unit used to break up
  reservation land. Primary: National Archives, Milestone Documents. This is the
  contradiction the grid carries; it informs the set's tone, not its imagery.
- **Center-pivot irrigation** — Frank Zybach's pivot (U.S. patent, 1952) produced the
  circle-in-a-square fields of the High Plains; the unwatered corners are the origin of
  the left eye's reading. Source: USPTO patent record; USDA/NASA aerial imagery for
  reference photography.

## Drawing and illustration lineages (the hand)

- **Survey-expedition artists** — Thomas Moran (Hayden Survey, 1871), Karl Bodmer
  (Maximilian of Wied, 1832–34), Richard and Edward Kern: field drawings made *for* an
  official record. Our 1/40 inherits their field-note honesty, not their romance.
- **Natural history** — Mark Catesby, John James Audubon (*Birds of America*,
  1827–38), the botanical plates of U.S. government surveys (Pacific Railroad Reports,
  1855–60). Lithographic density and the specimen vignette for the 1/20.
- **Sporting illustration** — A. B. Frost, Frederic Remington's pen drawings for
  *Harper's*, Charles M. Russell's letters with pen sketches. The 1/10's standard of
  animal drawing — and its warning about sentiment.
- **Land art and walking** — Richard Long and Hamish Fulton (the walk as the work),
  Agnes Denes (*Wheatfield — A Confrontation*, 1982), Robert Smithson. The 1/40's
  station walk is a walk-as-work.
- **The grid as feeling** — Agnes Martin, whose grids were made in New Mexico; Sol
  LeWitt's instructions. The proof that a grid can carry emotion rather than cancel it.
- **Seriality** — On Kawara (date paintings), Monet's grainstacks, Hokusai's *Thirty-six
  Views*. Same subject, many conditions: the logic of the 1/4 and 1/40.
- **Aerial looking** — the Center for Land Use Interpretation's archive; Ed Ruscha's
  aerial parking-lot photographs. The 1/1's vantage.

- Cole Calfee's red-ink sheets (e.g. `site/assets/RD_tattoo 1 1.png` in this repo):
  iron-oxide red on ivory, fast, scribbled vignettes. Paper and ink colours for the
  studies were sampled from it (paper ≈ #F0E8D9).
- Cole's PBR photography (`site/assets/pbr-bull.webp`) — source for WO-10-03 *The Chute*.
- Cole's paintings and ranch photography (Habitat Ranch, Teton Valley Ranch) — candidate
  sites for the drawn section and the forty.
