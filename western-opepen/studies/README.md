# Twelve composition studies

**These are AI/procedural composition studies, not artwork.** Each is stamped as such
along its left margin. They were generated by `../tools/make_studies.py` (seeded and
reproducible) on the exact Opepen geometry to test composition, density and
silhouette behaviour across all six groups. They are diagrams of decisions. The final
works are drawn by hand, and none of these will be traced.

Contact sheet: [`contact-sheet.jpg`](contact-sheet.jpg).

| Study | Group | What it tests | What the hand drawing must do better |
|---|---|---|---|
| S01 One Square Mile | 1/1 | Can the figure be *land* and still read at 64 px? Yes: pivot rings + row field + farmstead + grid. Creek through the neck. | Real place, real fields, varied line weight, earth-ink wash. The procedural version is too even and too clean. |
| S02 Breaking | 1/4 | Furrows obeying each form's geometry. | Furrow spacing should vary with the plow's fatigue; the team needs to be drawn, not glyphed. |
| S03 After the Blow | 1/4 | Can the figure survive near-erasure? Stubble dashes alone hold it. | Snow as untouched paper: restraint. The hardest piece in the group. |
| S04 Dwelling | 1/5 | Silhouette as void in a structure, horizon visible through it. Strongest graphic read in the set. | Board grain from observation; the openings' edges torn, not die-cut. |
| S05 Fence | 1/5 | Spare: the figure as a claim in wire. | Posts should lean; strands sag between them; one broken strand. |
| S06 The Pens | 1/10 | Corral plan: round pen, square pen; cattle crowd into the left eye's square corner. | Animals drawn as animals at small scale (sporting illustration, not seeds). |
| S07 Gather | 1/10 | The herd builds the silhouette by count. | Directional drift and bunching from life; the riders matter. |
| S08 Prairie Sunflower | 1/20 | Engraving vignette whose edge is the figure; specimen breaks the boundary. | Botanical accuracy from a real plant; hatch weight modelling the leaves. |
| S09 Nest | 1/20 | Figure survives only as pencil construction; specimen in the right eye. | Grass and egg speckle from a real nest (photographed, left in place). |
| S10 Sta. 1 | 1/40 | Horizon in the neck gap; land drawn through a faint figure; marks heavier inside. | Drawn on site in under an hour; the pressure change is the idea. |
| S11 Sta. 17 | 1/40 | Weather as the only mark; figure as denser rain. | Real rain on the paper is allowed. |
| S12 Sta. 31 | 1/40 | One continuous line at night; extreme spareness. | Must be genuinely drawn in the dark. Connectors will be wilder — keep them. |

## 64 px avatar test (Opepen is mostly seen small)

| Reads clearly | Reads faintly | Fails |
|---|---|---|
| S01, S02, S04, S08 | S03, S05, S06, S12 | S07 Gather, S09 Nest, S10 Sta. 1, S11 Sta. 17 |

This is the most important result of the first round. The **dense, value-based** pieces
hold at any size; the **spare, line-based** pieces disappear, and the system leans on
spare work in the 1/20 and 1/40. Consequences, now written into the editorial standard:

1. Every piece needs a **value contrast** between figure and ground, not only an
   outline. In a spare piece that can be a single heavy brush stroke, a pressure change,
   or one area of dense hatching — but it must exist.
2. In the 1/40 the construction figure is laid in with a **brush-weight line**, not a
   hairline. The "faint figure" in S10 was the wrong call.
3. Herd and rain pieces (S07, S11) must hold a hard edge where the figure meets the
   ground: density can't fade across the boundary.

Judgment: S01, S04, S08 and S02 are the system's strongest proofs. S03 and S05 are close
cousins (both boundaries); if both survive, they must look nothing alike when drawn.
